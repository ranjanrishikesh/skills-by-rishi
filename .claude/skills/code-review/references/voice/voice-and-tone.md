## The essence, in one line

**Write like the founders talking straight at one smart operator who has been getting away with doing it the hard way.** Speak as "we", the people who actually build this. Sure of the point, and not shy about naming what is broken or who is selling snake oil. Builders who have shipped this stuff telling you the truth, with the occasional "hell" when it is earned and a dry line where it lands. Never a brochure. Never mellow. Run it hot.

---

## The three pillars

Every sentence passes all three. They are filters, not moods to pick from.

### 1. Unmistakable (uniqueness + edge)

Say the thing only agentclaw would say. Category language ("streamline your operations", "cutting-edge AI solutions") is invisible; every competitor's site says it, so it says nothing. Replace it with a concrete specific: a real number, a named tool, an actual moment from the work.

- **Do:** name the invoice count, the Monday spreadsheet rebuild, the "3 flagged for you" moment. Specifics are the differentiator and the proof.
- **Don't:** reach for a phrase a competitor could paste onto their own site unchanged. If they could, cut it.
- **Before:** "We deliver innovative AI solutions that streamline your business operations."
- **After:** "37 supplier invoices come in overnight. By 9am they are coded and posted, and your bookkeeper reviews the 3 exceptions instead of typing all 37."

### 2. Provocative confidence  *(corrected — was "quiet confidence", which read mellow)*

Pick a side and say it out loud. Name what is broken, and who profits from it staying broken. Make the operator who has been coasting a little uncomfortable, because that comfort is exactly why the spreadsheet still owns their Monday. **This is bold. Not mellow.**

The guardrail that keeps it authority and not a tantrum: **every shot is earned by a real point underneath it.** We back the edge with substance: the number, the build, the system we actually shipped. Confidence you can check.

**Calibrated 2026-07-13 (founder): run the edge hotter. When a line could go bolder or safer, go bolder. The heat floor is higher than the home page's first draft.**

- **Do:** make hard declarative claims and let them stand. Take the side. Admit the limit directly when it is real ("some spreadsheets are innocent"; "if these things are true, hire someone instead of us").
- **Don't:** hedge into mush ("this might potentially help somewhat"), stack intensifiers with nothing under them, or fence-sit to sound balanced. Neutral reads as mellow, and mellow is the one thing this voice is not.
- **Before:** "AI can be a valuable tool that may help improve certain business processes over time."
- **After:** "Most 'AI strategy' is a slide deck that dies in a drawer. We build systems that are running by the third week. That is the whole difference, and it is not close."

### 3. Spoken founder cadence (the founders' "we")

It reads like it was *said*, not drafted. Speak as the company: **we**, the founders and the people who actually build this. "We built these systems inside our own companies first." Varied rhythm — a three-word sentence next to a long one that wanders through an aside and lands somewhere you did not expect. Contractions, fragments, the occasional "And" or "But" to open a sentence. **Dry, deadpan humor.** And the occasional mild swear (hell, damn, crap) when it actually lands.

> This **supersedes** the old "short declaratives" instruction. Short declaratives are the punch, one tool in the kit. A page written only in short declaratives reads like a machine set to staccato. The voice is the *mix*: punches and long conversational runs, together.

- **Do:** vary sentence length hard. Use the concrete verb ("carries data between them by hand") over the abstract one ("facilitates data transfer"). Let a dry aside land.
- **Don't:** reach for first-person "I" (the site speaks as "we", the founding team; "I" is reserved for the bylined blog), write in the formal register ("utilize", "in order to", "furthermore"), or make every paragraph the same length.

## The foils — what the voice pushes against (all four, founder-selected)

An edgy voice defines itself by what it attacks. Name these, do not tiptoe around them:

1. **AI hype & fake tools** — the chatbot demos and "AI strategy" theater that never ship a system that runs on Monday.
2. **The reader's own inertia** — the spreadsheet rebuilt every Monday, the hire they keep delaying, work done by hand forever because nobody stopped to fix it.
3. **Expensive consultants & big agencies** — slow, pricey, all slides, nothing in production.
4. **Headcount over output** — hiring your way out of a problem AI should have absorbed.

**The one rule that keeps this from backfiring: punch at the pattern, never at the reader.** The reader is the smart operator we are on the side of. The foil is the thing that has been quietly wasting their people. Make them feel caught, then hand them the way out — never make them feel stupid.

## Register policy (founder ruling, uniform)

**Uniform full founder voice on every page type — the register never tightens.** A blog comparison, the services page, and the terms of service all speak in this same voice as the blog. There is no "polished-neutral agency mode" anywhere on this site.

**The one invariant the voice operates within:** voice governs *register*, never *facts or function*.
- **Legal pages:** founder voice, yes — every statement stays legally accurate and complete.
- **Data / UI microcopy:** founder voice where there is room; clarity of the action wins any conflict.
- **Person (calibrated 2026-07-13):** the site speaks as **"we"** (the founding team). First-person "I" is reserved for the blog, which carries a personal byline.
- **On swearing across pages:** it is occasional by definition. Legal pages and microcopy simply have fewer moments where a "hell" earns its place. Do not force it in, and do not scrub the voice out either.

Same voice everywhere. The invariants are the frame; the voice is the paint. Never sacrifice the frame for the paint.

## Hard rules (non-negotiable)

1. **No em-dash. Ever.** Not `—`, not `–` used as one, not a spaced `--`. Do not swap it for a comma or en-dash — **rewrite so no dash was ever needed** (split into two sentences, or fold the aside into the main clause). The flagship AI tell; hard-blocked by the pre-publish gate.
2. **No curly quotes or apostrophes.** Straight ASCII only (`"` and `'`). A curly glyph in a straight-quote repo is a paste-from-model fingerprint; also hard-blocked.
3. **No AI-hype vocabulary:** revolutionize, unleash, supercharge, elevate, unlock, seamless, robust, leverage, game-changer, transformative, cutting-edge, delve, tapestry, testament to, "in today's fast-paced world". (Full living list: the tells catalog.)
4. **No exclamation marks** in body copy. The edge comes from the claim, not the punctuation.
5. **Mild swearing is allowed** (hell, damn, crap) — seasoning, never every line, never the substance. If the swagger is not backed by a specific, cut the swagger, not just the swear.
6. **Talk about *their* team's output, not our technology.** The client's people are the hero of every sentence.
7. **en-US spelling** everywhere.
8. **Concrete over abstract, specific over general** — the antidote to every AI tell at once.

The full, living catalog of AI tells and their founder-voice rewrites lives at `ai-tells-catalog.md`. This document sets the voice; the catalog is the detailed detect-and-rewrite reference.

## Enforcement (the important part)

This voice is **not** applied while drafting. During normal work — building pages, writing content, writing code — the agent works normally, like any AI agent. The voice and the humanizer apply **at the publish boundary only**: on `git push` or a PR.

At that point, automatically:
1. The deterministic **pre-publish gate** (`.claude/hooks/humanizer-gate.mjs`) hard-blocks the push if any banned token (em-dash, curly quote) remains in shipped content.
2. The **rewriting pass** (`rewriting.md`) rewrites the changed content into this voice and strips every AI tell.
3. **Code review** runs on the same diff; its findings get fixed.

See `rewriting.md` and the `PreToolUse` hook in `.claude/settings.json`.

## The 10-second voice test

Before any copy ships:

- [ ] Could a competitor paste this sentence onto their site unchanged? → not **unmistakable**. Add a specific.
- [ ] Em-dash, curly quote, hype word, or exclamation mark? → rewrite the sentence's need for it.
- [ ] Read it aloud. Does it sound *said* or *drafted*? → if drafted, break the rhythm.
- [ ] Does it take a side, or is it fence-sitting to sound balanced? → neutral is mellow. Pick the side.
- [ ] Is there a real point under the attitude? → if the swagger has no specific under it, cut the swagger.
- [ ] Is the client's team the hero, or is our tech? → make it theirs.
